Background

CI&T is a global digital transformation company serving large enterprise clients. Enterprise digital transformation projects often come with long sales cycles, averaging around 1.5 years. For marketing, the real challenge was not simply reaching more people, but staying relevant throughout a long decision-making journey, building trust over time, and helping sales teams move high-value accounts forward.

In the China market, I led the design and rollout of CI&T's Account-Based Marketing strategy from the ground up, turning broad marketing outreach into a structured, tiered, and collaborative relationship-building system.

The goal was clear: scale our reach while making every key account feel genuinely understood and thoughtfully engaged.

The Challenge

Although CI&T had strong industry credibility and global case studies, expanding high-value enterprise accounts in China came with several challenges:

Long sales cycles required tighter alignment between marketing and sales.

Marketing outreach to strategic accounts was still too generic to build deep trust.

Enterprise buying committees were complex, making it risky to rely on a single contact within an account.

The team needed a repeatable approach for account planning and relationship nurturing.

This meant marketing had to move beyond campaigns, events, and lead generation, and become more involved in account research, relationship building, and pipeline creation.

Strategy & Execution

I built the ABM framework around a "structured relationship plan," covering target account selection, persona mapping, touchpoint design, and sales collaboration.

1
Defined the Ideal Customer Profile

I created the target account selection criteria across multiple dimensions, including company size, ownership structure, local decision-making autonomy, digital maturity, business challenges, industry priority, executive access, and promoter signals.

This helped the team shift from "any company could be a prospect" to a clearer view of which accounts had the highest strategic value and deserved deeper investment.

2
Built Account Tiering and Customization Models

I segmented target accounts into A / B / C / D / X tiers, with different levels of customization mapped to each tier.

High-priority accounts received more personalized engagement, including executive involvement, tailored content, private sessions, VP-signed notes, and curated book gifting.

Mid-priority accounts were nurtured through vertical-specific content, targeted event invitations, webinars, direct email, and always-on content engagement.

This tiering model helped the team allocate resources more effectively, maintaining broad coverage while focusing the deepest effort on the most valuable accounts.

3
Mapped Key Contacts and Decision Roles

I classified stakeholders within target accounts into three persona groups:

P1 CEOs, CDOs, digital transformation leaders, and core decision-makers
P2 CMOs, CIOs, CHROs, and directors across marketing, IT, and HR
P3 Managers and functional influencers across marketing, IT, digital, supply chain, legal, and related teams

Each persona had different responsibilities, pain points, and influence within the buying journey. Based on this mapping, I developed messaging and value proposition frameworks to help sales tailor conversations to different decision contexts.

4
Designed Multi-Channel, Multi-Touch Relationship Nurturing

I turned ABM from a single outreach activity into a continuous relationship-building journey.

Key actions included:

  • Using LinkedIn Sales Navigator to identify decision-makers and influencers within target accounts.
  • Sending CI&T-produced ebooks, industry insights, and case study content through LinkedIn InMail to key contacts, using thought leadership as an early engagement touchpoint instead of direct sales messaging.
  • Using social listening to track customer moments such as awards, promotions, company anniversaries, and market news, then responding with timely and personalized congratulations.
  • Running LinkedIn account-based advertising to reach selected target accounts with content relevant to their business challenges.
  • Sending curated physical books with personalized executive notes based on the customer's specific context and challenges.
  • Inviting target customers to speak at CI&T events or join industry conversations, creating more natural opportunities for relationship building.

The principle behind these actions was simple: we did not treat customers as names on a list, but as real people and organizations facing specific business challenges.

5
Aligned Sales and Marketing Around Account Growth

To make ABM operational, I established regular collaboration routines with sales and growth units, including weekly meetings, PDCA reviews, target account updates, and campaign performance reviews.

I also encouraged executives to act as relationship sponsors for strategic accounts, bringing executive networks into the customer engagement process.

The approach started as a pilot in one growth unit and was later adopted by three growth units, becoming part of the team's regular sales and marketing collaboration.

Impact

This ABM strategy helped the CI&T China team shift from campaign-driven marketing to a more systematic account-driven growth approach.

  • The pace from initial touchpoint to meaningful business conversation became noticeably faster.
  • Several target accounts reached C-level engagement.
  • The team expanded coverage of key contacts within target accounts, enabling more consistent long-term nurturing.
  • Customer feedback often reflected a sense of being understood and valued, strengthening relationship depth.
  • The ABM methodology moved beyond the pilot stage and was actively adopted by multiple internal teams.
  • Personalized engagement around key customer moments, such as company anniversaries, awards, and role changes, created more meaningful connections than standard business outreach.
  • The initiative became a representative internal case for ABM and structured relationship nurturing at CI&T, and was shared in team and marketing practice discussions.
"ABM is not just a set of tools. It is a marketing mindset: use data to focus, content to create relevance, and relationships to create opportunity."
Reflection

This project deepened my belief that B2B marketing is not only about generating visibility or leads. It is about helping an organization choose the right customers, build relationships with more intention, and become a trusted partner when those customers are ready to move.

For me, ABM is not just a set of tools. It is a marketing mindset: use data to focus, content to create relevance, and relationships to create opportunity.
