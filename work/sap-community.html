Background & Challenge

For enterprise software companies, marketing is about far more than brand awareness.

Unlike consumer products, enterprise solutions often involve complex technologies, long decision-making cycles and multiple stakeholders. Before adopting a product, customers need to learn, explore best practices, seek peer recommendations and build confidence in both the product and the surrounding ecosystem.

For SAP, community was not simply a content distribution channel. It was a critical infrastructure connecting customers, developers, partners, technical experts and internal product teams.

At the time, SAP sought to strengthen its local community presence in China by bringing together a fragmented network of users, partners, developers and industry influencers into a more connected and engaged ecosystem.

The challenge was clear:

How could SAP Community Network evolve from a content destination into a thriving community that encouraged participation, knowledge sharing and long-term ecosystem engagement?

Approach

As the Community Evangelist of SAP Labs China, I executed regional community strategies in the local market. My responsibilities spanned community operations, user engagement, gamification initiatives, online-to-offline programs, influencer relations, content marketing and performance reporting.

1
Driving Engagement Through Gamification

To encourage ongoing participation and knowledge sharing, I supported the local implementation of gamification mechanisms, including points, badges and contributor ranking systems.

Community activities such as posting, commenting, answering questions and participating in events were translated into visible recognition and progression paths. These mechanisms helped increase participation, encourage long-term engagement, identify high-value contributors and establish a stronger recognition system within the community.

For key contributors and opinion leaders, I leveraged tiered engagement programs, contributor recognition and content visibility initiatives to strengthen their influence and encourage continued participation. Over time, the community developed a stronger culture of peer-to-peer knowledge sharing driven by its most engaged members.

2
Connecting Online Communities with Offline Events

I supported the execution of major SAP technology events, including SAP TechEd, D-Code, CodeJam and Hackathons, and served as a SAP Community Network Ambassador during these initiatives.

Rather than treating events as standalone marketing activities, we positioned them as entry points into the broader community ecosystem. Through event registration programs, post-event content distribution and ongoing discussions, we transformed one-time event participants into longer-term community members.

The conversation did not end when the event ended. The community became the place where knowledge sharing, collaboration and relationship building continued.

3
Building a Content and Influencer Ecosystem

Alongside community operations, I supported a broad range of content and communication initiatives, including social media management, newsletter production, video content development, SEO programs and user research projects. I also worked closely with technology journalists, industry influencers, developer advocates and technical experts to strengthen the community's reach and credibility.

By combining official content, expert perspectives and third-party amplification, we increased the visibility of the community while reinforcing SAP's position within the local technology ecosystem.

4
Demonstrating Community Value Through Data

To ensure community initiatives remained aligned with business objectives, I tracked key performance indicators such as user growth, engagement levels, content contributions, community participation and event-to-community conversion. These insights were translated into regular reports for management and business stakeholders, helping them better understand the role of community in customer education, ecosystem development and brand building.

The data also informed future optimisation efforts and helped strengthen internal support for community investment.

Impact

This initiative contributed to the development of a more active and influential technology community for SAP in China. It helped establish a localised operating model that connected customers, developers, partners and industry influencers through a shared platform for learning and collaboration.

By combining gamification, contributor engagement programs and community-led knowledge sharing, the project encouraged members to evolve from passive content consumers into active participants and contributors. It also strengthened the connection between online communities and offline technology events, transforming one-time interactions into longer-term relationships.

Over time, the community became an increasingly important vehicle for product education, ecosystem engagement and trust building, while regular reporting helped make its value more visible across internal stakeholder groups.

Key Takeaways

For complex enterprise products, customers often trust communities before they trust brands.

The most valuable communities are not simply content channels. They are trust networks that connect users, experts, partners and product teams around shared knowledge and experiences.

When content, events, incentives and expert participation are intentionally connected, communities evolve beyond marketing assets and become part of the broader business ecosystem. They help users solve problems, learn from peers and build confidence in the solutions they adopt.

Years later, while working on corporate branding, thought leadership, sports sponsorships and AI-driven marketing initiatives, I have found that many seemingly different marketing disciplines ultimately address the same challenge:

How do you build trust in complex decision-making environments?

Technologies change. Channels evolve.

But trust remains at the heart of effective marketing.

And communities remain one of the most enduring ways to build it.
